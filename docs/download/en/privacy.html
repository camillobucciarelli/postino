Privacy policy

Last updated: 16 November 2026. It covers the Postino app (macOS, Android, iOS) and this website.

In short

  • Postino has no mail server of its own: the app talks directly to the providers you connect (Google, Microsoft, iCloud, your IMAP server).
  • Your mail stays on your device, encrypted. So do your credentials; if you turn on synced settings, an encrypted copy also goes to your Google Drive, which only your devices can read.
  • The app has no analytics, no ads, no automatic error collection and no push notifications from a server. The one exception: on Android, when you scan a QR, Google's ML Kit sends diagnostic data to Google (see Camera).
  • The developer receives your data only if you send them a problem report yourself; the website and downloads go through Cloudflare (see below).

Where your data stays

  • Mail. Headers, message text, labels, drafts and outgoing messages are stored in a database on your device, encrypted with SQLCipher. The key is generated at random on first launch and kept in the system keychain (Keychain on macOS and iOS, Keystore on Android).
  • Attachments. The ones you download sit in an encrypted cache on your device, with a separate key in the keychain. When you open an attachment in another app, Postino writes a plain copy to a temporary folder and deletes it later.
  • Credentials. Google and Microsoft access tokens and the passwords of iCloud and IMAP accounts are in the system keychain and are used only with the provider they belong to. With synced settings on, an encrypted copy also goes to Google Drive (see below).
  • Backups. On Android, Postino's data is excluded from cloud backup and from transfer to a new phone. On iOS the database key stays on this device only: a backup may contain the encrypted database but not its key, so your messages cannot be read from a backup. After a restore on another iPhone or iPad you connect your accounts again and your mail syncs again from the providers. On macOS this protection is not in place yet: the key is in the login keychain, encrypted with your Mac password, which can end up in Time Machine backups and move to a new Mac with Migration Assistant. Account access tokens and passwords, and the key of the downloaded attachments cache, stay in the Keychain, which may be included in Apple's encrypted backups: they stay protected by that encryption.

Who the app talks to

  • Google, for Gmail accounts: sign-in with Google's consent (accounts.google.com, oauth2.googleapis.com), reading and sending mail with the Gmail API (gmail.googleapis.com) and a connection to imap.gmail.com used only to learn when new mail arrives. Postino asks Google for full access to your mail (https://mail.google.com/) and for your email address. When you remove the account, Postino revokes its access at Google.
    Postino's use of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. In practice: Gmail data serves only the features you see in the app (reading, searching, organising and sending your mail, and automatic labelling, which runs on your device), is not transferred to third parties, nor used for advertising, nor to train artificial intelligence models. The developer does not receive it and no person reads it, except what you choose to send in a problem report (see below).
  • Microsoft, personal accounts (Outlook.com, Hotmail, Live) and work or school ones (Microsoft 365): sign-in with Microsoft's consent (login.microsoftonline.com) and mail through Microsoft Graph (graph.microsoft.com). Permissions requested: read and change mail, send mail, basic profile and ongoing access. When you remove the account the authorization stays in your Microsoft account: you can revoke it in your Microsoft account settings or, for a work or school account, ask your organization's administrator.
  • iCloud and other IMAP/SMTP servers: the servers you enter. For an IMAP account whose servers you do not enter, Postino looks them up. Your password is never sent in this lookup. It asks for the configuration at autoconfig. followed by your address's domain, and then by its mail server's domain: this request includes your full email address. Requests that contain your address or the domain travel only over encrypted connections (HTTPS), never plain ones; if the lookup gives no valid configuration, you enter the server settings by hand. It looks up the domain's mail server with Google's DNS service (dns.google.com), which receives only the domain, plus your device's IP address. It checks Mozilla Thunderbird's public configuration database (autoconfig.thunderbird.net) with the domain only. Finally it tries to connect to the domain's most common server names: it opens direct connections to those servers, even unencrypted ones, but sends neither your address nor your password.
  • Google Drive, only if you turn on synced settings and choose a Gmail account for syncing. Postino asks for access to the app's own hidden folder only (drive.appdata), not to your files, and saves there one file with: the automatic labels configuration, swipe actions and, for each connected account, its address, provider, colour, position and credentials: for Gmail and Microsoft the refresh token and the permissions granted, never the access token; for iCloud and IMAP the password and servers. Never your mail. The whole file is encrypted with AES-256-GCM and a random 256-bit key, the sync key, which stays in the keychain of your devices and never goes through Drive: it passes from one device to another by pairing, scanning a QR or typing a code. Neither Google nor the developer can read the file. If you lose a paired device, use “Regenerate the key” from another device and change or revoke the credentials at the providers.
  • Hugging Face (huggingface.co), only when you first turn on automatic labelling: Postino downloads the model that then runs on your device. Message content does not leave the device.
  • Camera: only for “Scan the QR” when pairing, and only if you allow it. Postino neither saves nor sends the images. On Android the QR is read by Google's ML Kit, through Google Play services: when you scan a QR, ML Kit sends Google information about the device and the app, device and installation identifiers, performance metrics and error codes, for diagnostics and usage analytics, over an encrypted connection and without passing them to third parties (ML Kit's data disclosure). On iOS and macOS the system reads the QR. You can type or paste the code instead.
  • Updates on macOS, in the version downloaded from the website: Postino reads the list of versions from postino.camillobucciarelli.dev, the developer's website, and downloads updates from there; on the second launch it asks whether to do so automatically.
  • Updates on Android and iOS, on Android only in the APK downloaded from the website: at most once a day, and when you choose “Check for updates”, Postino reads a small file with the number of the latest version from postino.camillobucciarelli.dev, over an encrypted connection. The request is the same for everyone: it carries no personal data and no identifier, beyond the technical data of every connection described below. If there is a new version Postino tells you; the download (from the website on Android, from the App Store or TestFlight on iOS) only starts if you choose it.
  • Remote images: blocked by default. If you choose to show them, for one message or always for a trusted sender, your device downloads them from the servers named in the message, which can thus tell that you opened it.
  • Links: open in the system browser.

Notifications

Notifications are created on your device: Postino checks for mail in the background (on Android about every 15 minutes, or in real time with immediate notifications; on iOS when the system allows it) and uses no push notification service.

Error log and problem reports

Postino keeps an encrypted, size-limited error log on your device; tokens and passwords are removed before writing, while message subjects and addresses may remain. The log never leaves the device by itself. If you choose “Report a problem”, or accept the offer after a crash, Postino prepares a draft addressed to the developer with the log in its text: you read it and decide whether to send it. If you have not connected an account yet, Postino lets you share the log file through the system share sheet.

Removing an account

When you remove an account, Postino deletes from your device its mail, drafts, outgoing messages, automatic labelling data and credentials, and empties the attachment cache. With synced settings on, the account and its credentials are also removed from the file on Drive and from the other paired devices. Nothing changes at the provider: your mail stays where it is.

Controller and the developer's processing

Data controller: Camillo Bucciarelli. Privacy contact: camillo@bucciarelli.dev.

Mail that stays on your device and travels between you and your providers does not pass through the developer: for that mail the developer processes no data at all. Your relationship with Google, Microsoft, Apple and other providers follows their own privacy policies.

The developer processes personal data only in these cases:

  • Problem reports. If you send a problem report, the developer receives your email: address, text and error log, which may contain subjects and addresses of your messages. Legal basis: your request, to answer and fix the problem. The report stays in the developer's mailbox and is deleted within 12 months of the report being closed; you can ask for it to be deleted sooner, at any time.
  • TestFlight invitations. To let you try Postino in preview on iPhone and iPad, the developer uses the email address you give them to add you to the test group on Apple's TestFlight. Legal basis: your request. The address stays in the group as long as you are in it and is removed when you leave the group or ask for it.
  • Website and downloads. To serve these pages, the downloads and the list of updates, Cloudflare receives the technical data of each request, such as the IP address and the type of browser or app, and keeps it according to its standard retention, described in Cloudflare's privacy policy; the developer sets no different period. The developer adds no cookies and no analytics. Legal basis: the legitimate interest in offering the website and the downloads.

You can ask for access to your data, rectification, erasure and restriction of processing, and object to processing, by writing to the contact above. You also have the right to lodge a complaint with the Italian data protection authority (Garante per la protezione dei dati personali).

This website

These pages use no cookies, analytics or third-party content: even the fonts are served from here. The website and the downloads are hosted on Cloudflare.
